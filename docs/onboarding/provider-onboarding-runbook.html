Internal · Ops runbook

Onboarding a new client business

Everything from "contract signed" to "client sending real WhatsApp messages", in order, with the reasons behind each step. Owned by the ops team; the client-facing half of this process is the companion guide "Getting your business onto WhatsApp" — send them that, keep this one.

Version 1.0 · September 2026 · applies to the AZentis Notify admin console · typical end-to-end time: 1–2 working days (dominated by the client's Meta setup and template review, not by us)

0Before you start

Collect these once, up front — chasing them mid-flow is where onboardings stall:

  • Client's business details — legal/display name, business category (clinic, shop, restaurant…), logo file, brand color if they have one.
  • One named contact on the client side with authority over their Facebook Business assets — this is who will do (or approve) the Meta setup steps.
  • The phone number they'll send from — confirmed not active on personal WhatsApp or the WhatsApp Business app. A number can't be in two places; if it's in use, they must either pick a different number or delete the existing WhatsApp account on it first.
  • Which integration they want — portal only (their team logs in and works in the UI), API (their backend sends via our REST API), or both. This decides whether step 7 applies.
Why the phone number question first: it's the single most common blocker. Everything else in this runbook can proceed in parallel, but a number tangled up in a personal WhatsApp account stops the show at step 3 — surface it on day one.

1Create the tenant

Admin console → Tenants → New tenant

  • Name — the client's business name as customers should recognize it.
  • Slug — lowercase letters, numbers, hyphens only (e.g. sunrise-dental). It's an internal identifier; short and obvious beats clever.
Why tenants matter: a tenant is a hard isolation boundary. Credentials, contacts, message history, templates, API keys — nothing crosses between tenants, ever. Getting a client their own tenant is what makes it safe to run many businesses on one platform.

2Branding & business category

Tenant page → Setup → 1 Branding

  • Upload the logo, set the primary color, write a one-line business description.
  • Set the business category — Healthcare, Retail, Restaurant, Services, Education, or Other.
Why the category isn't cosmetic: it decides which starter message templates the client sees first in step 4 — a clinic gets appointment reminders front and center, a shop gets order updates. Skipping it means the client wades through irrelevant templates.

3WhatsApp credentials

Tenant page → Setup → 2 Credentials → "Enter credentials manually"

The client owns their Meta/WhatsApp assets; we operate them. The client-facing guide walks them through producing exactly four values. Request them with something like:

Copy-paste request to the client contact
Hi — to connect your WhatsApp number to the platform we need four values from your Meta setup. The attached guide ("Getting your business onto WhatsApp", section 2) shows where each one lives, with screenshots-level detail:

1. Phone Number ID  2. WhatsApp Business Account ID  3. Permanent access token  4. App Secret

Please share them through a password manager link or an encrypted note (not plain email or chat) — the token and secret are the keys to your WhatsApp account. We'll confirm the connection with a live test message the same day we receive them.

When the values arrive:

  1. Paste all four into the Credentials form. The WABA ID is technically optional, but always ask for it — without it, template sync and template submission (step 4) can't work.
  2. Click Verify with Meta. This makes a live call against Meta's API before anything is saved — you should see the business's verified name and phone number echoed back.
  3. Save. Credentials are encrypted the moment they're stored; nobody, including us, reads them back out in plain text.

Handle secrets like secrets. Delete the client's message containing the token once saved, and never re-share it onward. If a token ever leaks, the client revokes it in Meta Business Settings and issues a new one — then you repeat this step.

3b. Point the client's webhook at us (needed for replies & STOP)

In the client's Meta app: WhatsApp → Configuration → Webhook. Have them (or do it with them on a call) set:

  • Callback URL: https://<our-api-domain>/v1/webhook/whatsapp
  • Verify token: generated when you saved credentials (shown in the save confirmation; retrievable by ops afterwards).
  • Subscribe to: messages.
Why this can't be skipped: without the webhook, we still send fine — but customer replies, delivery/read receipts, and automatic STOP/START opt-out handling never reach us. An inbox that never fills up and consent that never updates is a compliance problem, not a cosmetic one.

4Templates

Tenant page → Setup → 3 Templates

  1. Open the Starter templates gallery — it opens on the group matching the business category. Pick the 2–4 the client actually needs and hit Submit to WhatsApp on each. They go to Meta for review pre-filled and correctly formatted.
  2. If the client already has approved templates on their WABA, click Sync from Meta to pull those in too.
  3. Watch the status column: PENDING → APPROVED usually within minutes to a day. Re-sync to refresh.
Why templates before test send: WhatsApp only allows free-form messages inside a 24-hour window after a customer writes in. To message anyone proactively — the entire point of reminders and alerts — an approved template is mandatory. Template approval is the longest single wait in onboarding; start it early so it overlaps with everything else.
If a template comes back REJECTED: most rejections are category mismatches (promotional wording in a UTILITY template). Adjust the wording in WhatsApp Manager or pick the MARKETING starter instead — and remember marketing templates must keep the "Reply STOP to unsubscribe" line; every starter marked Marketing already includes it.

5Test send — prove it end to end

Tenant page → Setup → 4 Test send

  1. Send a test to your own phone first, then to the client contact's phone.
  2. Have the recipient reply to the message — then check the tenant's Inbox page. The reply appearing there proves the webhook (step 3b) is wired correctly. This is the only cheap moment to catch a broken webhook; after go-live it surfaces as "customers say they replied and nobody answered".

6Portal access for the client's team

Tenant page → Workspace → Portal access

  • Create one portal account per client team member (email + temporary password — have them change it at first login).
  • Send them the portal URL: https://<admin-domain>/portal. What they get: dashboard, campaigns, contacts, templates, inbox, analytics. What they never see: credentials, API keys, other tenants.
Why there's no self-signup: portal accounts are created only by us, deliberately. It keeps account provenance auditable and means a client's WhatsApp channel can't be accessed by anyone we didn't explicitly create an account for.

7API keys — only if their backend integrates

Tenant page → Setup → 5 API keys

  • Issue a key, share it the same secure way as step 3 (it's shown once, then only its prefix).
  • Their backend calls POST https://<api-domain>/v1/send with Authorization: Bearer <key> — same guardrails as the portal: opt-in checks, quiet hours, rate limits all still apply.

8Go-live & the first two weeks

  • Templates APPROVED, test message delivered, test reply visible in Inbox, portal login confirmed by the client.
  • Client has imported (or started importing) contacts with opt-in consent — see the consent section of the client guide. We enforce opt-in at send time; contacts without it simply won't receive messages.
  • Watch Analytics → Sending health for the first campaigns: the daily unique-customer ceiling (250/day until the client completes Meta business verification) and the quality rating. A warning banner appears automatically at 70% of the daily limit.
  • If they'll outgrow 250 customers/day: start their Meta business verification now (client guide, section 6) — it takes days, not hours, so don't wait for the wall.
Why quality rating is the number to respect: Meta scores every number by blocks and reports from recipients. High quality raises the sending ceiling automatically; poor quality lowers it and can pause the number. The two levers that keep it green are genuine opt-in (step 8, item 2) and honest, expected content — both are client behaviors, so set the expectation during onboarding, not after a downgrade.
