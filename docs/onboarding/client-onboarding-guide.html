1What you'll need before starting

  • A Facebook account that belongs to you or someone senior in the business — it will own your business's WhatsApp assets.
  • A phone number for your business's WhatsApp that can receive an SMS or call right now, and — important — is not currently used with the WhatsApp app on anyone's phone. A number can only live in one WhatsApp system at a time.
  • Your business details: name, address, website or social page.
  • About 45 minutes, ideally on a laptop rather than a phone.
About that phone number: if you want to use the number your customers already know, and it's currently on the WhatsApp app, talk to us first. Moving it is possible but it deletes the app account in the process — that deserves a planned decision, not a surprise.
Why this setup exists at all: WhatsApp requires every business to have its own official WhatsApp Business Account with Meta. It's what makes your messages arrive with your business name instead of an anonymous number — and it's why this is a one-time setup, not something you'll ever repeat.

ACreate your Meta business portfolio

Go to business.facebook.com → log in with your Facebook account → Create a business portfolio (if you don't already have one)

Enter your business name, your name, and a business email.

BCreate an app on Meta's developer site

Go to developers.facebook.com → My Apps → Create App → choose the Business type → name it after your business → connect it to the portfolio from step A

Don't worry about the word "app" — you're not building software. This is just Meta's way of packaging API access.

CAdd WhatsApp and your phone number

Inside your new app → find WhatsApp in the product list → Set up → open API Setup

Click Add phone number: enter your business name (this is the display name customers will see — Meta reviews it, so make it your real business name), your business number, and confirm the code Meta sends by SMS or call. The screen you land on afterwards looks roughly like this:

DCreate a permanent access token

business.facebook.com → Settings → Business Settings → Users → System Users → Add

  1. Create a system user (name it e.g. whatsapp-platform, role Admin). A "system user" is a robot identity — it keeps working even if staff change or someone's Facebook password resets.
  2. Click Add Assets → Apps → select the app from step B → enable Manage app.
  3. Click Generate New Token → pick the app → set expiry to Never → tick the permissions whatsapp_business_messaging and whatsapp_business_management → Generate.

FSend us the four values — securely

  • Use a password manager's share feature or a one-time secret link (we'll suggest one if you don't have a preference). Please don't paste them into a normal email or WhatsApp chat — the token and secret are literally the keys to your WhatsApp account, and email lingers forever.
  • We'll confirm receipt, connect your number the same day, and delete your message once the values are stored (they're encrypted on our side and never displayed again).

3What happens next — our side

  1. We verify the connection live against Meta before saving anything — you'll get a confirmation that your business name came back correctly.
  2. We set up your message templates. WhatsApp requires pre-approved templates for any message you send proactively (more on why below). We submit ready-made ones for your kind of business — appointment reminders, order updates, offers — and Meta usually approves them within a few hours.
  3. We send a test message to your phone, and ask you to reply to it. Your reply appearing in your inbox on our platform confirms the full two-way loop works.
  4. You get your portal login — see section 5.
Why templates are a thing: WhatsApp lets a business message anyone freely only within 24 hours of that person's last message to you. Outside that window — which is exactly when a reminder or delivery update happens — WhatsApp requires a pre-approved template. It's their spam defense, and it's also why messages from businesses on WhatsApp feel trustworthy rather than junky.

4Your customers must say yes first — consent

This is the one rule that protects everything else. Here's exactly what happens to a contact, start to finish:

Contact added
CSV import or signup
Opted in
Messages flow normally
Replies STOP
Blocked automatically

A later reply of START moves them straight back to "Opted in" — no action needed from you either way.

  • Only message customers who agreed to receive WhatsApp messages from you — a checkbox on your booking form, a signup sheet, a verbal yes you record. When you import contacts, you're confirming they opted in.
  • The platform enforces this at send time: a contact without consent simply won't receive messages, even if they're on a campaign list.
Why this is in your interest, not just a rule: Meta scores your number by how often recipients block or report you. A good score raises your daily sending allowance automatically; a bad one shrinks it and can pause your number entirely. Consent isn't paperwork — it's what keeps your channel alive and your messages welcome.

6Your sending limit, and when to think about verification

Every new business starts at a fixed daily allowance. It's 250 different people a day, not 250 messages — for most businesses that's plenty to start:

  • If you expect to contact more than ~200 different customers a day soon, complete Meta business verification (uploading registration documents at business.facebook.com → Security Centre). We'll flag it in your dashboard when you're approaching the limit — and we can walk you through it.
